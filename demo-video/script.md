# Surge Desk: narration script (English, ~112 s)

**problem.** A fire breaks out in a Hell's Kitchen apartment building. In two minutes, New York's nine one one gets twenty six calls. Most describe the same fire. Hidden among them are people who will die if they wait in line. Meet Surge Desk.

**surge.** Calls arrive as live transcripts with the phone's location, in English, Spanish, Mandarin, Bengali and Russian. Each one is judged in about a second. Watch the counters: calls keep climbing, but the number of real emergencies stays small.

**jev.** This is Jev, TypeSafe's decision model. It answers six typed questions per call with calibrated probabilities. Is this the same emergency as an open incident? What does it add? How severe is it, and is a life at risk right now? Code turns those answers into a priority.

**hidden.** Here is why it matters. A man not breathing on Fifty Second Street. A cyclist hit by a cab, because everyone was watching the fire. A seventy eight year old with chest pain at the corner. They mention the fire, but Jev splits them out as separate emergencies.

**intel.** And a duplicate is never thrown away. A neighbor in a wheelchair still in six C. A silent text: smoke in eight F, with a six year old. Those become radio updates for the crews already on scene. Nothing moves until the supervisor approves.

**ivr.** Then the supervisor drafts a known incident message. Callers about this fire hear it first, in their own language. Jev checks every claim against the facts before it plays. Press one if your emergency is different.

**callbacks.** Dropped calls are ranked by what's heard in the background, so coughing and a crying child get the first callback.

**close.** Twenty six calls, six real emergencies, and the critical ones first. The AI proposes, and the human approves. That's Surge Desk.
